It beat the baseline.
Then I audited it.

An LLM-driven AutoML loop searching for predictive signal in Kalshi prediction markets, starting from transformer sentiment embeddings. Its best run beat the momentum baseline by +0.023 F1. That result was leaked, and finding out why is the actual contribution.

The question

Does news sentiment carry residual signal a market hasn't priced in?

Prediction markets are supposed to absorb public information quickly. If freely available news sentiment still predicts short-term price moves, that is a small crack in market efficiency. The second question was methodological: can an LLM-driven loop discover useful feature families on its own, without being told what to look for?

The loop

A reasoning agent proposes; trial subagents write the code.

Each iteration is a hypothesis with a paper trail. The acceptance gate is the part that matters — it is deliberately hard to pass.

The audit

The best feature the loop found was memorizing the test set.

Data-integrity finding

An early run reported F1 = 0.4179, apparently beating momentum by +0.023, and the gain traced to market-structure features (market_cat_freq, event_hour) the loop had proposed unprompted. A satisfying result: the agent found something a human hadn't specified.

It was leaked. Kalshi renamed ticker prefixes between the train and test windows — CPI became KXCPI — so only 2 of 32 test prefixes existed in training at all. The frequency encoding wasn't learning market structure; it was distinguishing test rows from train rows.

Diagnosing it, rebuilding a KX-normalized encoder, and re-running under purged temporal CV recovered the honest result: no edge over momentum. The paper reports the null.

Future work

What the paper says to do next.

Three changes, each aimed at a specific way this run was weaker than it should have been.

  1. 01

    Use temporal cross-validation from the first iteration

    The early loop ran on a fixed validation split, peaked at F1 = 0.373, and collapsed to 0.333 on test. Temporal CV was adopted mid-project as a repair. Starting there would have made every iteration's number comparable and caught the overfit far earlier.

  2. 02

    Put price-based features in the initial feature set

    Rolling volatility, momentum, and bid-ask proxies were never given to the loop as starting material, so it searched sentiment space alone while the baseline it had to beat was itself pure momentum. Seeding the price features lets the search find sentiment signal conditional on them rather than competing against them.

  3. 03

    Optimize for statistical alpha, not raw F1 gain

    F1 is the wrong objective for a market question. A model that is rarely confident but well calibrated when it is — exactly what the confidence-gated result describes — scores poorly on average F1 while being the more useful object. Restructuring the AutoML objective toward statistical alpha would search directly for that.
